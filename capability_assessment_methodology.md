# Capacity Assessment Methodology

## Metadata
- Established: 2026-10-23; last revised: 2026-11-18; version 2.6 (reusable-coach adaptation).
- Scope: general evidence/inference rules, with detailed modules for strength, aerobic fitness, and hiking. Select only modules relevant to this user's goals. These modules are not a complete assessment system for every sport, body-composition goal, or clinical situation; use appropriate sourced methods for new domains before numerical inference. Use existing records and feedback; no additional in-session recording.
- Facts: training_log.md; background: profile.md; prescriptions: training_plan.md; conclusions: current_status.md.
- Literature supports assessment principles. Comparison tiers, question budget, aggregation windows, and judgment wording are project conventions; individual prediction accuracy is unvalidated.
- Aim: the most reasonable, actionable, revisable informed guess from available information. Imperfect evidence still calls for synthesis, rather than merely repeating recent/best results or listing what cannot be confirmed.

## 1. Assessment Targets

Select dimensions and success measures from profile.md. No hiking-first hierarchy, strength test, or endurance metric is required for every user. When no baseline exists, label capacity not yet established and use normal training to establish a relevant working point. Units follow the user's profile; examples below are illustrative, not user data.

Assess working capacity under specified conditions/effort and, where supported, infer potential capacity, trends, and likely next performance. Maintain dimensions separately. Completed results support inference; they are not automatic ceilings.

| Dimension | Indicators |
|---|---|
| Strength working capacity | Exercise variant, load × sets/reps, RIR, quality, repeatability |
| Aerobic working capacity | Mode, speed/distance, duration, heart rate, RPE/breathing |
| Durability | Timing and magnitude of changes in late-session output, effort, and control [14][15] |
| Across-day recovery/repeatability | Daily-function recovery window and subsequent comparable training |
| Technique/control | Quality, side differences, and limitations at a specified difficulty |

## 2. Model and Error

**Y (d, t) = f_d[C (d, t), X (d, t)] + U (d, t) + ε(d, t)**

- Y: observed performance; C: task capacity; X: task conditions; U: day-specific state/strategy; ε: observation/reporting error.
- The model reminds us that capacity, conditions, and daily state jointly affect performance. Combine history, reserve, movement feel, and recovery to identify likely capacity/limitations. Inability to separate factors precisely does not preclude directional judgment. Do not invent contribution percentages or fit fitness–fatigue time constants to sparse data. [13]
- Treat heart rate as accurate by default; interpret with temperature, grade, exercise progression, and physiological state. [8]
- Treat RPE as ordinal, without proportional changes. Preserve RIR intervals and judgment error.
- Sets/laps share the day's state. Only independent training days count as repeated verification.
- No cross-activity composite scores, unsupported personal success probabilities, or contextual correction coefficients.

## 3. Data Extraction

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Task | Exercise/equipment/ROM, activity mode, route, carried load |
| Output | Weights/reps, distance/time, existing laps, ascent/descent |
| Internal response | RIR, RPE, heart rate, breathing, local sensations |
| Termination/quality | Planned finish, fatigue stop, time limit, technique breakdown, pain |
| Context/aftereffects | Known prior load, state changes, subsequent function |

- Leave unknowns unfilled, rather than zero/normal. Do not create duplicate raw logs.
- Confirm variants once, then check only changes.
- Do not require extra set-level RIR, timed rests, talk tests, lap marking, valid-rep counts, videos, or morning measurements.
- Use summaries when laps are absent. Reduce precision/state assumptions when data are limited; nonessential missing fields do not automatically prevent judgment.

## 4. Inference and Updating

### 4.1 Comparability

| Tier | Conditions | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Directly comparable | Same task, similar effort/quality, no major known changes | Compare numbers/repeatability while retaining day-to-day variation |
| Partly comparable | Duration, order, strategy, or environment differs | Synthesize output, effort, and history; rough estimates allowed, without equating all performance change to pure capacity gain |
| Different task | Substantial mode, variant, terrain, or load changes | Maintain separate baselines |

Lower confidence for important unknown conditions; retain branches only if they could reverse the conclusion. Imperfect matching does not justify refusing all comparison. Do not assign arbitrary comparability weights or uncalibrated sleep/weather corrections.

### 4.2 Intervals and One-Sided Information

- Planned-duration completion demonstrates tolerance. Finish reserve, repeated performance, and recovery can support longer-task expectations. Separate completed volume, predictions, and unknown limits.
- RIR 3+ means ≥3. q=3 may serve as a conservative model input; other evidence can support an explicitly assumed scenario, whose upper bound must not masquerade as measured RIR.
- Fatigue termination means that session's target was not completed, not an objective physiological limit. A time-limited finish is not fitness failure.
- Limit local-function feedback to its activity, exercise, and conditions. Judge calf raises, running, and squats separately; one reaction does not prove others are limited. Unreported does not mean pain-free/recovered; unknown does not mean impaired.
- Bound recovery timing between successive observations of the same function. With dates alone, use calendar-day windows.

### 4.3 Updating Process

1. Extract output, effort cost, quality, and aftereffects; match recent comparable sessions.
2. Evaluate each indicator as improved/unchanged/declined, then synthesize the most likely capacity conclusion. Interpret volume, effort, and quality together rather than mechanically upgrading by tonnage/distance.
3. Weigh main alternative explanations: best fit and conflicting evidence. Add sensitivity analysis/questions only when they could reverse the decision; do not list every theoretical possibility equally.
4. One high-quality performance can update capacity and the next prescription. Repeated comparable results strengthen stable trends; no extra observation week is required.
5. For a single decline, first update that day's performance. Review volume/recovery for repeated declines with fatigue/function problems.
6. For conflicts, inspect sources, task differences, and recency. State the most credible explanation; widen ranges/split tasks as needed, without forced averaging or attributing all poor performance to fatigue.
7. Retain observation dates without new evidence. After detraining, illness, or task changes, downgrade prior values to historical references.

Trends depend on comparability, magnitude, and replication, not a fixed minimum session count.

### 4.4 Aggregation

- With sparse data, retain Session conditions/ranges; no regression.
- For matched repeated performances, the latest 3–5 median/full range may describe results. Do not average across progression weights or treat the range as a confidence interval/prescription.
- Account for trends, familiarity, and contextual differences before estimating personal noise. Training-period SD/CV is not automatically measurement error. [1]
- No universal progress percentage before individual error calibration. Multiple sets do not remove systematic RIR/formula error.

### 4.5 Informed Guess — Judgment and Confidence

- **Lead with judgment, then evidence.** Answer current level, main limitation, and whether to advance, supported by key performances. Raw results or “cannot prove” do not replace coaching judgment.
- **Separate four information types.** Observations are completed facts; model estimates include formula-derived e1RM; coaching judgments are synthesized best estimates; unknowns lack even a useful rough anchor. Specific estimates/judgments must not be backfilled as user/device reports.
- **Estimate when anchored.** Recent interpretable same-exercise load/reps/reserve can support provisional e1RM. Repeated easy completion with reserve supports the next duration. Missing maximal tests, precise rests, or full environmental data does not automatically invalidate estimation. Decline reliable numerical inference only for unrelated evidence, excessive extrapolation, or material conflict, and offer the most useful narrower judgment.
- **Give points/ranges distinct roles.** Give a central estimate when defensible, then useful rough ranges/scenarios. Otherwise give direction/branches without forced numbers. Explain input/assumption/coaching origins; do not claim statistical confidence or that formula sensitivity includes all errors.
- **Use high/moderate/low confidence with reasons.** High: recent repeated comparable consistent evidence, robust to major assumptions. Moderate: directly relevant evidence with some conversions/assumptions. Low: many assumptions, distant history, or large extrapolation. Repetition does not eliminate formula/self-reported-RIR bias; high confidence in work sets does not transfer automatically to 1RM. These are qualitative, not calibrated success probabilities; no need to label every sentence.
- **Assumptions are allowed when explicit.** Keep original unknown RIR unknown. If nearby sessions support a plausible estimate, use it only in estimation rationale and check decision sensitivity. Do not calculate when no reasonable assumption exists.
- **Decide capacity and prescription separately.** Do not understate likely capacity to express caution. Continue demonstrated tolerable work and progress on output/quality/reserve criteria. Add margins only for task limitation or materially changed failure consequences. Low confidence, unconfirmed recovery, or mild soreness does not automatically require reductions.
- **Use predictions for calibration.** State expectations testable in normal training. Revise after misses and change assumptions/models after repeated misses. No maximal testing or extra check-ins to validate guesses.

## 5. Strength

### 5.1 Working Capacity

Task parameters: **load w, sets S, reps r, target reserve q, movement quality, order/rest context**.

| Change under comparable conditions | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| More load, same sets/reps, quality, reserve | Signal of improved load capacity |
| More reps, same load, quality, reserve | Signal of improved capacity in that rep range |
| Same output/quality, more reserve/easier | Supports improvement; one-session RIR difference of 1 is weak evidence and needs history |
| More load with less reserve, shorter ROM, or longer rest | Assess whether added cost explains output. Working load rose; pure strength change needs separate estimation |

- First sets reflect less local fatigue; last sets reflect maintaining the whole prescription.
- Treat overall RIR at Session level, without filling individual sets. Tonnage is not strength.
- Later pull-up rep loss informs volume, reserve allocation, and fatigue, but its ratio does not establish fresh maximum reps. Changed assistance creates different task conditions.

### 5.2 e1RM and Sensitivity

**Brzycki: M = w × 36 / [37 − (r + q)]**. [2][3]

q is RIR; adding RIR extends application of the original repetition formula.

For clearly defined usable main lifts such as squat, bench, and standing barbell press, actively assess e1RM usefulness and estimate when suitable data exist; lack of measured 1RM alone does not justify omission. Select a recent representative set, identifying set-specific versus exercise-wide RIR. Overall RIR permits rough estimates, not claims of measured set RIR. Later-set fatigue, order, and individual repetition–load differences matter; do not automatically compensate to a fresh maximum.

**q_true = q_reported + habitual bias b + session error e**; retain additional individual repetition–load and exercise-variant formula error. [4][9][10]

| Demonstration: squat 155 lb×5, reported RIR 2–3 (not current status) | Input q | e1RM |
|---|---|---|
| Reported | 2–3 | 186–192 lb |
| ±1 repetition | 1–4 | 180–199 lb |
| ±2 repetitions | 0–5 | 174–207 lb |

- ±1/±2 are uncalibrated assumptions. Results exclude formula error and are not confidence intervals for true maxima.
- Example: “I estimate this lift's 1RM around 190 lb (moderate confidence), from 155×5 at RIR 2–3. With ±1 RIR error, formula scenarios are roughly 180–200 lb, plus unquantified formula/technique error.” Lead with the estimate, not just 155 lb 3×5.
- r+q≤10 is the project's preferred low-rep conversion range, not a physiological hard boundary. Beyond it, do not force this formula; use an applicable sourced model or same-exercise history for low-confidence estimation, or retain working-capacity judgment.
- RIR 3+ permits a conservative q=3 e1RM scenario, not a strict lower bound on actual 1RM. Open RIR alone cannot establish a closed e1RM range. Anchor broader estimates in other bounded same-exercise performances rather than inventing a 3+ ceiling.
- With missing RIR, consider plausible assumptions from nearby matched sessions/feedback without inventing the original value. Use a few conditional scenarios if decision-relevant; their midpoint is not automatically the best estimate.
- For face pulls, unclear row variants, dumbbell RDL, and tasks lacking suitable definitions/conversions, favor working-capacity assessment. Do not convert machine readings, single-dumbbell loads, or added pull-up weight into generic 1RM. Estimate only with a clear variant and suitable model. [10]
- Usually round e1RM to a practical load increment in the user's preferred unit (for example ~5 lb or ~2.5 kg). Separate input/RIR sensitivity, formula error, and practical coaching ranges; do not impose a fixed ±percentage to manufacture a range. [4][9][10]
- Historical 5RM is conditional background, not a basis for exact recovery percentages against current multiset submaximal work. Do not prescribe from e1RM upper bounds or turn estimates into maximal tests.

## 6. Aerobic Fitness

### 6.1 Working Points

Record **mode/conditions—distance/speed—duration—heart rate—RPE/breathing—termination reason**, synthesizing matched recent training, late-session changes, and recovery. Separate task performance, easy continuous capacity, and physiological parameters such as VO₂max. One unknown does not invalidate others; completed duration is not a ceiling.

Actively estimate easy pace, sustainable target duration/distance under similar conditions, and trends. Estimate VO₂max, thresholds, or race performance when suitable models/inputs exist. Missing maximal tests alone is not a reason to refuse. When essential model inputs cannot reasonably be assumed, provide task-capacity judgment instead. [18][19][20]

### 6.2 Laps

- Use existing laps, matching exercise phase, distance/time, and terrain; exclude obvious warm-up, stops, and unmatched segments.
- State segment-selection rules; do not cherry-pick best laps.
- **Speed v = 3600 / pace in seconds**, km/h. Calculate output change using speed.
- Do not fit personal heart-rate–speed slopes with confounded lag, grade, and variable pace.
- Laps are not independent retests; no default heart-rate-normalized regression.
- Heart-rate decoupling needs stable continuous same-mode, comparable terrain after warm-up. Do not calculate by default or apply a 5% threshold.

### 6.3 Interpretation

| Change under roughly comparable conditions | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Similar duration, more output, no higher HR/effort, unchanged quality | Improvement signal |
| Similar output, lower HR, no higher effort | Possibly lower cost; check conditions |
| Longer/faster with higher RPE/HR | More completed work; assess likely capacity change from cost, difficulty, and history |
| Lower HR but slower and more tired | Do not call improvement; review fatigue/conditions |
| Same output, easier breathing, better next-day function | Improved task-tolerance signal |

- Jointly lower HR/effort at matched pace/time, or more output at similar cost, warrants “aerobic performance likely improved,” not mere data repetition because conditions differ. Lower confidence for sufficient weather/route/intensity/state explanations. For conflicts, state the favored explanation/reason. Group submaximal-HR validity is not personal longitudinal precision. [11]
- Identify the best-supported main limitation—breathing, local muscle tolerance, pacing, or durability—as coaching judgment. A single late HR rise does not diagnose poor cardiopulmonary fitness, dehydration, or a mechanism.
- Breathing/leg sensations clarify limitations, without extra differentiated RPE. [16][17]
- Do not infer conversational ability without reported conversation or add fixed test segments.

### 6.4 Inference Targets, Boundaries, and Confidence

Apply section 4.5; this is not a personally calibrated grading scale. Moderate confidence is neither a fixed success rate nor a required session count.

| Target | Evidence and permissible conclusion | Confidence / extrapolation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Repeating similar tasks; easy pace | Recent matched mode/route, time, pace, RPE/breathing, termination, recovery; judge reliable task magnitude and practical pace range | Can be high with consistent repetition/recovery; one session permits provisional judgment. Pace applies to similar conditions, not all weather. A best kilometer is not whole-run capacity |
| Longer time/distance | Completed volume, finish reserve, late stability, recent training/recovery; predict next similar task as feasible, demanding, or presently inadvisable | Usually moderate near demonstrated volume with reserve; lower for larger extrapolation, unknown aftereffects, or simultaneous speed/elevation/load increases. Target completion does not establish maximum duration |
| Development trend | Direction across comparable/partly comparable output, HR, RPE, recovery; judge improvement/stability/decline | Can be high with consistent evidence/small confounding; partial matching usually moderate. One better session does not quantify pure aerobic gain |
| Easy intensity relative to VT1 | Natural conversation, RPE, breathing, sustained performance; judge suitability for easy base work and cautiously near/below VT1 | Confidence in subjective ease may be high, VT1 location usually moderate/lower. Standard talk-test research supports approximate location; casual conversation does not identify precise threshold HR/pace. HRmax is unnecessary for easy-intensity judgment [5] |
| VO₂max | Applicable submaximal model, recent device estimate, or related test; identify mode, algorithm/source, date, inputs; point or justified broad range | Usually moderate/low without personal calibration; depends on model fit, inputs, cross-evidence. Do not invent ratios from one mean HR/pace pair, reuse old cycling values as current running values, or call small device changes true gains [18][19][20] |
| Threshold HR/pace; critical speed (CS) | Applicable sourced model, sustained relevant intensity, device estimate, or higher-effort records at varied durations; conditional estimates | Moderate with matching model/task and sufficient intensity evidence. Easy-only data usually support relative location only. Define VT1, VT2, lactate threshold, CS separately. Natural training can inform CS, but not mechanical fitting of a few easy runs [5][20][21] |
| Race/target performance | Recent higher-effort same-mode results, distance differences, volume, durability; suitable model/coaching extrapolation | Usually moderate for nearby tasks with clear effort; lower for distant extrapolation/insufficient durability. Easy 5 km is not a maximal 5 km. Half/full-marathon predictions also need volume/durability; no cross-terrain/load/altitude guarantees [22] |

### 6.5 Confidence and Use

- **Confidence is conclusion-specific.** Evaluate session ease, ability to run longer, VO₂max, and next recovery separately. Reliable inputs do not guarantee reliable derivations; repeated HR/pace relationships do not remove model bias.
- **Narrow the question before refusing estimation.** Even without maximum duration, assess a specific target. Even without exact threshold, judge base-training suitability. Pause numerical inference only for absent anchors, unselectable key assumptions, or conclusion-reversing conflict, naming the gap.
- **State extrapolation conditions/distance.** Specify mode, route/grade, intensity, recovery assumptions. Nearer tasks/fewer assumptions raise confidence. Long easy work, short hard work, loaded hiking, and altitude are not interchangeable through one aerobic metric. Unknown recovery is neither normal by default nor automatic incapacity/reduction.
- **Numbers need traceable foundations.** State formula/algorithm source, inputs, assumptions, applicability, main errors. For proprietary devices, report outputs/context rather than pretending to reproduce algorithms. If HRmax is required but unverified, use an explicit population assumption with sensitivity checks. Observed peaks constrain compatibility; they are not automatically running HRmax, and conflicts with age formulas must not be ignored.
- **Research errors inform precision, not personal intervals.** Separate study mean error, meta-analysis agreement limits, formula sensitivity, and practical coaching ranges. Explain range inputs/assumptions. Historical tests are dated, mode-specific anchors, not licenses for arbitrary ±percentages. Prefer task judgment if a very broad number adds no decision value. [18][19][20]
- **Separate capacity/prescription.** Do not understate likely capacity or prescribe the prediction's upper edge. Use current plan, recovery, and progression criteria. Check completion, effort, and aftereffects in normal training; revise after deviations, without extra maximal/talk tests/check-ins.

### 6.6 Wording Examples

Illustrations only, not reassessments or fixed progression formulas.

- “Repeated easy 40–46-minute runs with reserve make 50 easy minutes under similar conditions realistic (moderate confidence). 60 minutes may be possible, with lower confidence because it extrapolates further.” This is reserve-based judgment, not calculated maximum duration/statistical interval. Give target-specific confidence rather than only “maximum unknown.”
- “Faster at similar effort, with a consistent recent direction: aerobic performance appears to be improving (moderate confidence). Weather may contribute, so the entire pace difference is not a pure-capacity percentage.” Keep the conclusion clear and only material limitations.
- “RPE 2–3 and comfortable conversation suit easy base training; likely near/below VT1 (moderate-to-low confidence), without identifying exact threshold HR.” Separate session intensity from physiological localization; assess model/input suitability if precision is needed.

## 7. Hiking

### 7.1 Task and Synthesis

Task conditions: **distance, ascent/descent, total/moving time, altitude, pack load, surface, late performance, recovery, consecutive days**. Match routes/terrain first; use recent strength, aerobic work, and recovery as supporting readiness evidence, not direct conversions into hiking distance, load, or altitude.

For a target route, actively estimate physical completion feasibility, difficulty, main limitations, duration, and next-day reserve, with confidence. Lack of an exact historical match does not preclude prediction. Completed routes are anchors, not ceilings. Judge known conditions first; ask only for missing details that could reverse the conclusion.

Separate one-time completion, easy/reserve completion, and next-day repetition. With imperfect matching, state likely completion, demanding finish, needed shortening/load reduction, or current inadvisability; do not merely list history/unknowns.

### 7.2 Inference Targets, Boundaries, and Confidence

Use section 4.5's high/moderate/low confidence. These are coaching guidelines, not calibrated readiness scores/probabilities.

| Target | Evidence and permissible conclusion | Confidence / extrapolation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Single-day route and tolerable volume | Similar distance/elevation/time/pack/reserve/recent state; estimate feasibility, demanding sections, reasonable time/elevation range | Higher with repeated recent matches; one relevant completion/nearby extrapolation usually moderate. Lower for old evidence, distant extrapolation, or multiple increases. Past completion does not establish current ease; longest record is not a ceiling |
| Route time | Personal same/similar-route speeds, climb/descent/technical sections, rests, late changes | Usually moderate with relevant splits, higher if repeated; lower with totals alone/unfamiliar terrain. Separate moving, expected elapsed, itinerary buffer. Sourced route formulas are scenarios, not personal calibration |
| Sustained climbing | Segment ascent/time, grade/surface, RPE/breathing, late maintenance; judge controlled intensity, target gain feasibility, vertical speed if supported | Moderate/higher with repeated similar climbs. Without segment timing, no false-precise m/h; cumulative gain/tolerance still assessable. Flat-running improvement informs readiness without converting to vertical speed |
| Descent/durability/main bottleneck | Late speed, leg sensations, control, voluntary/forced slowing, next-day function; judge local tolerance, prolonged fatigue, pacing, fueling | Moderate with consistent phenomena/feedback, higher with repetition, lower without local feedback. Select most likely limitation without equal-weight alternatives; slowing alone does not diagnose injury, a specific weak muscle, or exact decline breakpoint [7][14][15] |
| Pack/surface/technical changes | Walking/elevation at known loads, shoulder/back/foot response/recovery, related terrain experience; judge target load, shorter days, or reduced load | Usually moderate near experience; low for light-to-heavy packs or easy-to-complex terrain. Conditional judgments allowed; squat/RDL/VO₂max cannot calculate maximum pack capacity or certify technical terrain [6] |
| Consecutive/multiday itinerary | Next-day function, subsequent activity, repeated training, consecutive-day history; predict maintain/shorten/recover and overall feasibility | Moderate/higher with matched consecutive days. Single-day/recovery evidence permits provisional judgment, with declining confidence across days. Clear next-day limitation argues against repeating full volume, not all movement. One easy day does not certify 5–7 identical days |
| Fitness at different altitudes | Similar-altitude itineraries/acclimatization and current low-altitude training; conditional distance/elevation/load readiness | Less relevant altitude experience/larger difference weakens extrapolation. Assess fitness, acclimatization, technical demands separately. Low-altitude endurance/one high-altitude completion does not guarantee higher-altitude safety; no personal altitude-illness probabilities or invented correction factors |

### 7.3 Estimation and Use

- **Match major conditions without demanding identical ones.** Assess distance, elevation, pack, terrain, altitude, consecutive-day differences and which change the decision. Time-limited termination is not fitness failure. Totals, reserve, and recovery support initial judgment without full pace curves.
- **Use historical and recent evidence jointly.** Older long hikes anchor prior experience/completion. Judge transfer using recent output, detraining/illness, aftereffects, and changed conditions. Do not discard solely for age or equate recent strength/easy-running gains to restored long-hike capacity.
- **Source time ranges.** Identify route/segment, speed/model, rest assumptions. Where possible, estimate segment moving times then rests. Pure vertical speed = segment ascent / segment time; total ascent/elapsed time is not climbing speed. Use a few conditional terrain scenarios rather than exact distance-ratio projections or arbitrary fatigue percentages.
- **Give the best explanation of late limitations.** Match grade/surface/effort and distinguish voluntary/forced slowing. Manageable breathing with sore legs/declining control supports local/downhill tolerance as likely bottleneck. Add fueling, environment, prior fatigue only with evidence. This is revisable judgment, not diagnosis/contribution shares; unreported nutrition/water/environment does not establish deficiency. [15]
- **Separate extrapolation and prescription.** Estimates may exceed completed volume; route selection still follows recent load, recovery, goals, and failure costs. Unknown next-day effects neither default to normal nor invalidate all capacity. Judge single-day completion separately from next-day confidence. State expected time and itinerary buffer separately; do not deliberately understate capacity as caution.
- **Stop numerical inference only at critical gaps.** Pause a specific number for absent anchors, unknown decisive route conditions, or simultaneous large changes without plausible assumptions. Still give a narrower supported conclusion/adjustment; “insufficient evidence” is not the end of assessment.
- **Calibrate in normal outdoor training.** Compare predicted/actual time, late sensations, next-day walking/stairs/subsequent activity. Revise after repeated matched misses. Keep normal progression; no extra loaded/descent/consecutive-day tests or recording tasks for modeling. [6][7]

### 7.4 Wording Examples

Examples only, not current reassessment, route recommendations, or fixed formulas.

- “A similar completed route with finish reserve and normal next-day function makes a modestly longer day under the same pack/surface realistic (moderate confidence). Extra duration follows reserve/recovery; the previous finish was not a limit.” Give a specific target rather than only “longer capacity unknown.”
- “If breathing remains manageable while legs ache and downhill control falls, durability/downhill tolerance is likely the main bottleneck (moderate confidence). Control long descents before simply increasing running speed.” Test this working explanation during the next normal hike.
- “Completion with clearly limited next-day walking/stairs argues against repeating the full dose on day 2. A shorter second day is more reasonable (moderate confidence). Assess further days against the itinerary/cumulative recovery.” This neither certifies multiday readiness nor rules out all next-day walking.

## 8. Subjective Feedback

- Preserve wording/context; do not force “easy” into numerical RPE. [12]
- Separate overall RPE, muscle soreness, clear pain, and task limitation. Prioritize location, change, and functional effect; mild stable soreness alone does not trigger reductions/delay.
- Interpret RPE against purpose, sustainability, and aftereffects, not as a standalone reduction trigger.
- Missing next-day feedback means unconfirmed, neither recovered nor mandatory rest. Continue normal training and earned small progressions without fixed 48-hour/full-body recovery proof.
- Distinguish warm-up relief from full recovery. Pain-free normal function/stable target movement permits continuation; lack of proof of full recovery does not imply stopping.
- State timing relative to training; no fixed reporting times.
- Ask neutrally; accept “similar/cannot recall.” Missing reports are not zero load/normal recovery.

## 9. Follow-Up Information

Usually ask only 1–2 items, prioritizing safety/scheduling, then interpretation. Do not repeat answered/irrelevant questions. Add recovery at the next natural conversation, without extra check-ins.

| Trigger | Follow-up |
|---|---|
| Missing main-lift RIR | Final-set same-technique reserve band: 0–1 / 2–3 / 4+ |
| Cannot produce force / breakdown | Muscular inability versus awkward movement/support; pain |
| Rep drop between sets | Forced drop versus deliberate reserve; substantially shorter rests |
| Longer/faster aerobic work | Planned finish versus fatigue; breathing versus legs |
| Changed same-task response | Major route, stops, weather, pretraining-state differences |
| Late-hike slowing | Voluntary/forced, main limitation, food/drink |
| Next conversation after larger load | Current walking/stairs function, overall fatigue |
| First pull-up/single-leg baseline | Assistance, technique standard; platform height/support |

- Confirm variant only initially/after changes; do not demand precise recall.
- Safety gaps can exceed the usual question budget without bundling unrelated questions.
- Attach additions to original Sessions; no new session counts.

## 10. Forward Validation and Tools

- Give conditional expectations before training; compare output, reserve, quality, aftereffects afterward. Predictions are not mandatory volume.
- After repeated misses, revise task definitions/uncertainty rather than merely adding retrospective explanations.
- Before numerical modeling, fix outputs, limit parameters, disclose priors; validate forward in time against simple baselines such as recent matched performance. [13]
- Complex models need forward-prediction benefit; historical fit/decimal precision does not establish validity.
- Do not promise a fixed error level after a set number of sessions.
- `scripts/capability_sensitivity.py` calculates RIR scenarios, not capacity scores, success probabilities, or individual error intervals.
- Population rep tables can be priors cross-checked against personal same-exercise performance. Personal 1RM estimation is allowed; population means are not individual certainties/rankings. [10]

## 11. File Maintenance

- current_status.md: lead with synthesized capacity judgment; give best estimates such as e1RM where applicable, confidence, key support, material limitations, and next steps. Recent/best results support rather than replace conclusions. Do not create a separate personal evidence-boundary table that becomes stale.
- Log Analysis: briefly state changed judgments, likely explanations, and next-training implications. Retain decision-relevant limits without repeated disclaimers. Preserve historical judgments; explain new understanding in current status or dated follow-ups rather than pretending it was previously known.
- Separate observation, model estimate, coaching judgment, unknown. Date estimates and state necessary assumptions; e1RM needs brief sensitivity. Reference this methodology instead of repeating full derivations.
- If conclusions are unchanged, update relevant evidence, replication counts, or recovery, not appended session summaries.
- Full facts/corrections stay in the log; short-term fatigue in Recovery; prescriptions in the plan. Method updates do not add tests/rebuild blocks. “Pending” status/plan fields are not training bans. Reductions require user feedback/actual performance, not old prescriptions/coaching opinions alone.
